What’s happening

Where this industry stands right now.

Buyers are active in the sector

Business services, including IT managed services, accounting, and insurance, is one of the concentrated sectors for lower middle market acquirers in 2026, alongside home services and industrial distribution.

Boomer ownership is concentrated here

Professional services is one of the sectors with the highest concentration of baby boomer ownership, which means competitive dynamics around succession will be most acute in this space over the next decade.

Recurring revenue is what gets paid for

Contracted, recurring revenue is valued in an entirely different way than project work. Most service businesses have more of it than their financials actually show, because nobody has structured the reporting to surface it.

What it means for you

Where we focus in this industry.

The financial work that actually moves the needle here, rather than a generic checklist.

  • Revenue recognition on multi-month engagements needs to be consistent, or your margins look erratic.
  • Utilization and realization rates are the real health metrics, and they rarely appear on a standard P&L.
  • Receivables timing drives everything in a service business, and it belongs in a rolling cash forecast.
  • Recurring versus project revenue should be separated in your reporting long before anyone asks.

What accounting do professional services firms need?

Books organized by client and engagement, payroll for employees and contractors, and reporting on utilization and cash timing, which are the two things service businesses live or die on.

Yes. We work with staffing and recruiting firms, marketing agencies, IT and managed services providers, engineering and design firms, and consultancies.
